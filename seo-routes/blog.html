Frequently Asked Questions About Photo Geotagging

Does geotagging degrade image quality or compress pixels?

No. In standard formats like JPEG and TIFF, metadata is stored in dedicated header blocks preceding or following the image bitstream. When FreeGeoTagger updates or injects EXIF GPS tags, it performs binary surgery on the metadata segments without decoding or re-encoding the compressed pixel payload. The image retains 100% of its original sensor sharpness, resolution, and dynamic range.

Do social media platforms keep the GPS coordinates I add?

Do not assume a service preserves or removes every metadata field. Image processing and sharing behavior can vary by product, setting, and time. If embedded location matters, inspect the actual copy received by the other person; use a direct file transfer you control and still verify the result.

What coordinate system does photo EXIF metadata use?

The international EXIF 2.32 specification requires coordinates to be referenced against the World Geodetic System 1984 (WGS84) ellipsoid datum. Coordinates are encoded as three pairs of unsigned rational numbers representing degrees, minutes, and seconds, accompanied by ASCII hemisphere reference characters ('N', 'S', 'E', 'W').

Can I geotag photos without an active internet connection?

Not reliably. Processing is local, but some modules load when first used. Maps and searches require network access. Load and test the required workflow before relying on it away from a connection.
