How to Geotag Photos in Lightroom Classic with a GPS Track

By FreeGeoTagger · Published September 26, 2026 · Updated September 26, 2026

Quick answer: Import a recorded GPS track into Lightroom Classic's Map module, select photos from that session, correct any camera-clock offset, and match them against the track by capture time. Review pins before writing metadata; a track match is only as accurate as the track and clock alignment.

Lightroom Classic can associate photographs with coordinates from a GPS track log. The workflow depends on the camera clock and track timestamps lining up: import the GPX track in the Map module, select the corresponding photos, resolve time differences, match, review the map, and save metadata. Consult Adobe's current Lightroom Classic Maps module documentation, because controls vary by version.

Before matching: preserve a safe working set

Back up the original images and the GPX track. Confirm that both cover the same outing and that the track recorder had location permission and satellite reception. A GPX file is a sequence of time-stamped positions, not a record of every shutter press. The software estimates a photo's position by matching its capture time to nearby track points.

Check the camera date, time, and time zone. A camera clock that is 20 minutes fast can place an image at the wrong part of a route even when both files are individually valid. Don't adjust timestamps until you know the offset and have preserved originals.

Import and match the GPX track

  1. Import the images into the Lightroom Classic catalog.
  2. Open the Map module and load the GPS track log (GPX) for the same session.
  3. Select only photos from that outing and inspect their capture-time range against the displayed track.
  4. If the clock is offset, use Lightroom's track-matching time adjustment controls to align the camera times to the GPS timestamps. Apply a known offset consistently.
  5. Run the track-log match and inspect the resulting pins across the route—especially the first and last images, gaps, and stops.
  6. Save metadata to files or sidecars only after the placements look plausible and your backup is secure.

Review suspicious placements

A pin snapped to a track can still be wrong. Look for photos before the track began, after it ended, long pauses, tunnels, weak reception, and time-zone transitions. A walking route may place images on the nearest recorded point, not the precise side of a street. A long drive with sparse track points may be too coarse for a building-level location. Exclude unmatched photos instead of forcing a coordinate.

Use the GPS Finder or an EXIF reader to check a small exported sample. Lightroom catalog coordinates, XMP sidecars, and the pixels/files delivered to someone else are separate things; verify the copy that matters to your workflow.

When Lightroom is the right choice

Track matching is useful when you have a GPX log recorded during the shoot and many photographs taken along a route. It can save manual work and preserve a relationship between time and position. It cannot locate a photo without a reliable track, infer a place from its visual content, or make poor timestamps accurate.

If you know one correct point for a group, a simple batch workflow may be quicker; see how to bulk geotag a photo set. If you need only a few known coordinates in individual copies, use the browser geotagger.

Metadata, export, and privacy

After synchronizing metadata, export one test image with the settings you plan to use. Inspect its GPS fields and verify the marker. Some export presets remove location data; some sharing platforms create new versions. Keep a private archive with useful location records and make a separate privacy-safe export when publishing to the web. Exact coordinates can expose homes, private land, or sensitive subjects.

Quick troubleshooting

  • All pins are shifted: verify camera clock, time zone, daylight-saving setting and any applied offset.
  • Only some photos match: check whether their timestamps fall inside the GPX track interval.
  • Coordinates vanish after export: inspect export metadata settings and then examine the actual exported file.
  • Track is noisy: compare questionable points with another independent record; don't present a weak GPS fix as precise.

Frequently asked questions

Can Lightroom Classic geotag photos from a GPX file?

Yes. Lightroom Classic's Map module can load GPS track logs and match selected photos by their capture timestamps.

Why are Lightroom GPS pins shifted along the route?

A camera-clock or time-zone offset can make capture times misalign with GPX timestamps; verify and correct the known offset before matching.

Does a Lightroom track match guarantee exact location?

No. Position quality depends on track reception, point spacing, timestamp alignment, and the photo's actual capture time.
