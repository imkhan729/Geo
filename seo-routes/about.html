About FreeGeoTagger

By FreeGeoTagger · Updated September 26, 2026

FreeGeoTagger provides browser tools for adding, reading and removing photo GPS metadata. The purpose is simple: help you control the location information inside the image file you actually share.

Choose the right task

  • Geotag photos when you know the location and want to write it into a copy of an image.
  • GPS Finder reads an existing location. It cannot identify a place from the scene or recover coordinates that are absent.
  • EXIF Viewer helps inspect camera, time and location fields together.
  • Remove GPS creates a copy for sharing with fewer location details.
  • Batch geotagging applies coordinates across a queue, including filename-based CSV assignments.
  • Coordinate Converter translates coordinate notation; it does not improve the accuracy of a location measurement.

What happens to your photos

The image tools read and process files in your browser. Selecting a photo does not send its bytes to our server. Downloads are new files; keep the originals until you have checked the results.

This is not a promise that the whole website makes no network requests. Maps request tiles for the viewed area. Address searches and reverse lookups send place text or coordinates to map services. Page delivery and analytics also involve network requests. See the privacy policy for the distinction.

Known limits

The file limit is 20 MB per image, and practical batch size depends on device memory. HEIC conversion produces JPEG and can change image quality, color handling and metadata. The normal JPEG, PNG and WebP geotagging paths edit metadata without recompressing image data; unusual JPEG files may use a conversion fallback. Not every photo application displays GPS in every file format.

Maps, searches and code loaded on demand need connectivity. Fully offline use is not guaranteed. A successful GPS read-back confirms the stored numbers, not that the photo was taken there. Geotags are editable and are not proof of attendance, ownership or survey accuracy.

How our guides are maintained

Guides are published under the FreeGeoTagger name. Product explanations are checked against the implementation; instructions for other products link to their official documentation where available. Worked examples are labeled as examples, not presented as customer studies or field measurements. Software comparisons disclose that this site publishes one of the products being compared.

We distinguish documented behavior from recommendations and limitations. We do not promise a Google ranking improvement from EXIF tags, invent testing results, or use article length as evidence of quality. Dates change when the page is substantively revised.
