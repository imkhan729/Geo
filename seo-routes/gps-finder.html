Understanding GPS Metadata in Photos

Location data is stored in a dedicated GPS sub-directory inside a photo's EXIF metadata. The essential fields are latitude and longitude, each paired with a reference field recording the hemisphere — north or south, east or west. Many cameras also record altitude, a UTC timestamp for the satellite fix, and a compass bearing describing which way the lens was pointing.

Accuracy depends entirely on the device that captured the image. A modern smartphone with a clear view of the sky is typically accurate to within about 5–10 metres. Indoors, underground, or between tall buildings, the fix degrades and a phone may fall back on a cached position from earlier — which is one of the most common reasons a photo appears in the wrong place.

If a photo turns out to have no coordinates at all, that is usually because location services were switched off for the camera, because the file came from a platform that strips metadata on upload, or because it is a screenshot rather than a camera photo. Any of those can be corrected by adding the location yourself, and our guide on fixing wrong photo GPS data covers the causes in more detail.

EXIF GPS Tag Technical Reference

Under the EXIF 2.32 standard, GPS coordinates and capture telemetry are structured inside the GPS Info IFD (Image File Directory) using standard tag identifiers and data formats:

Tag ID Tag Name Format Example Value Description
0x0001 GPSLatitudeRef ASCII 'N' or 'S' North or South latitude hemisphere reference
0x0002 GPSLatitude Rational[3] 40/1, 46/1, 588/100 (40° 46' 5.88") Degrees, minutes, and seconds of latitude
0x0003 GPSLongitudeRef ASCII 'E' or 'W' East or West longitude hemisphere reference
0x0004 GPSLongitude Rational[3] 73/1, 58/1, 235/100 (73° 58' 2.35") Degrees, minutes, and seconds of longitude
0x0005 GPSAltitudeRef Byte 0 (Sea Level) or 1 (Below) Sea level elevation reference flag
0x0006 GPSAltitude Rational[1] 1540/10 (154.0 meters) Elevation measurement in meters
0x0007 GPSTimeStamp Rational[3] 14/1, 25/1, 30/1 (14:25:30 UTC) Time of satellite fix in coordinated universal time
0x001D GPSDateStamp ASCII 2026:09:11 Calendar date of GNSS satellite fix

Troubleshooting: Why Does My Photo Have No GPS Data?

If GPS Finder reports that no location was found, the most common technical causes include:

  1. Social Media & Messaging Stripping: Apps like WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, and Twitter strip EXIF metadata on upload to protect user privacy. Download original camera files directly instead.
  2. Camera Location Permissions Disabled: Location Services was switched off for the camera app during shooting in iOS Settings or Android App Permissions.
  3. Satellite Signal Obstruction: Indoors, basements, and urban canyons block direct satellite line-of-sight before a GNSS fix is acquired.
  4. Screenshots & Exported Graphics: Screenshots, Canva graphics, and exported Photoshop files lack camera sensor telemetry.
