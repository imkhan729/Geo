How to Add or Edit a Location in Google Photos

By Jimmy Dickinson · Published September 26, 2026 · Updated September 26, 2026

Quick answer: Google Photos can display and, for eligible items, let you add or adjust a library location. That does not guarantee the downloaded image contains EXIF GPS. Check Google's current limits, then inspect the exported file separately if embedded coordinates matter.

Google Photos can show a place associated with an image and may let you add or adjust a location for eligible photos. A location shown in the app is not proof that latitude and longitude are embedded in the downloaded file. If another app or recipient needs EXIF GPS, download a copy and inspect that file.

Add or edit a location in the Photos app

Open the photo, open its information/details panel, and look for the location control. Google changes app layouts over time, and available edits depend on how the location was added and the device. Follow Google's current Add, edit, or remove a location in Google Photos instructions for your platform.

Google documents important limits: some locations can be edited while camera-recorded coordinates cannot be changed or removed in Google Photos; availability can also vary when selecting several images. Read the current help page before relying on a specific control. On Android, Google's instructions say a photo must be backed up before adding a location in the app.

Library location versus embedded GPS

There are two different things a user might mean by “the photo has a location”:

  • Library or account information: Google Photos associates a place with an item for display and organization.
  • Embedded EXIF GPS: latitude, longitude and reference values are stored inside the downloaded image file.

These can agree, differ, or exist independently. Google's help also describes estimated locations based on landmarks and other signals; an estimate is not the same as GPS captured by the camera.

Check the image you actually need

  1. Download or export the photo from Google Photos using the current app or web controls.
  2. Keep the original and note whether the exported file was converted or renamed.
  3. Open that exact file in an EXIF reader such as the FreeGeoTagger EXIF Viewer or GPS Finder.
  4. Compare any coordinates shown by the file with the location shown in the Photos library.

If the library place is right but embedded GPS is missing, and you have a sound basis for assigning that location, you can add it to a copy with a photo geotagging tool. Don't equate a Google-generated estimate with a confirmed capture point.

Sharing privacy settings

A location visible to you in your library does not necessarily mean it will be exposed to every recipient. Google Photos has sharing controls and behaviors that depend on the sharing method; check Google's current location-sharing help before sending a sensitive picture. When exact location should remain private, review both app sharing settings and the metadata in the file you distribute.

Common problems

The app shows a location but my computer does not

The place may be library information or an estimate rather than an EXIF GPS tag. Check the downloaded file, not just the app display.

The location control is missing

The item may have camera-recorded GPS, may not meet Google's edit conditions, or your app version may present the feature differently. Consult Google's help for current eligibility.

The shared photo has no location

The sharing route may omit location details or create another copy. Inspect the recipient copy if retention is important; avoid sending sensitive coordinates just to preserve metadata.

Frequently asked questions

Does a location in Google Photos mean GPS is embedded in the file?

Not necessarily. The location may be library information or an estimate; inspect the downloaded image's EXIF to confirm embedded coordinates.

Can I edit camera GPS in Google Photos?

Google's current help documents limits: camera-recorded locations cannot be edited or removed there, while some other locations may be editable.

Can Google Photos add a location to several pictures?

Some location edits support multiple selected photos, but camera-added data and current eligibility can limit the action; check Google's current instructions.
